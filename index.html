More than the fruit

Every avocado has more than one market

Fresh Hass fruit is the main market. When export prices dip, or fruit is too small or marked for export, it can still be sold to these other users.

Fresh fruit

Export markets in Europe, the Gulf and Asia, plus supermarkets, hotels and open-air markets at home.

Avocado oil

Cooking oil and a base for cosmetics. Kenya's output roughly tripled between 2024 and 2025.

Pulp and processed food

Frozen pulp, guacamole and ready meals use fruit that doesn't need to look perfect.

Skins, seeds and prunings

Can go back into the orchard as compost and mulch, which cuts fertiliser costs.

How it works

From seedling to harvest, in six steps

  1. 1

    Choose your tree

    Pick a tree that's already planted, or reserve one at the pre-seed price before planting. Each tree has its own ID, block and row, registered in your name.

  2. 2

    We plant and tag it

    We plant before we sell. A grafted Hass seedling from a certified nursery goes into prepared ground. Its tag, GPS point and first photo go on the register.

  3. 3

    The farm team cares for it

    Watering, feeding, pruning, pest control and security, all included. You never get a separate bill for care.

  4. 4

    It gets checked, at random

    Field verifiers photograph trees with location and time stamps. A second, randomly chosen verifier rechecks a sample.

  5. 5

    We harvest and sell

    From about year three or four, fruit is picked, graded and sold to export, local and processing buyers.

  6. 6

    You share the harvest

    60% of your tree's fruit is yours. Take it as fruit or as sale proceeds. You can also pass your tree on to someone else.

The harvest share

One simple split, paid in fruit

We don't charge monthly fees. The farm company earns 40% of the fruit your tree produces, and that covers everything it costs to grow it.

  • The 40% covers land rent, water, labour, inputs, security and harvesting.
  • If costs run higher than the 40%, the farm company carries the shortfall, not you.
  • In a poor season, everyone's share is smaller. We'll show you the harvest figures for every tree.

Take fruit

Collect your fruit at the farm, or have it delivered at cost.

Take proceeds

We sell your share with the rest of the harvest and pay you its sale value.

Verification

You can check that your tree is there

Every tree, including the reserve, sits on a register with its own history. Verification doesn't rely on taking our word for it.

Photo, place and time

Each check is a photo taken on the farm, with GPS and a timestamp. A check taken more than 50 m from the tree's pin is rejected.

Two verifiers, one random

A field verifier checks first. A second verifier, picked at random, rechecks a sample. If the two disagree, the tree is flagged for a visit.

Visit any time

Owners can book a farm visit and walk to their own tree. So can anyone you send.

One record, start to finish

Planting, checks, replacements, harvests and transfers all go on your tree's record.

Before you join

What can go wrong

Trees are living things, and farming carries risk. These are the main ones.

Trees can die

Drought, pests or disease can kill a tree. The reserve replaces losses, but a large loss could use it up.

Harvests vary

Weather and the age of the tree change how much fruit it gives. Some years will be thin.

Prices move

Avocado prices rise and fall with the season, exchange rates, shipping costs and export rules.

Pre-seed trees wait for planting

Land, weather or nursery supply can delay planting. If a pre-seed tree isn't planted within 90 days, you get your money back.

Nothing is guaranteed

We don't promise a fixed amount of fruit or money, or that a tree will be worth more later. The yield table is an estimate.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can I buy a tree today?

Not yet. We're asking Kenya's Capital Markets Authority how the model should be set up before we take any money. Join the list and we'll tell you when we open, and on what terms.

Where is the farm?

We are securing land in Laikipia County on a 30-year lease. We'll publish the location once the lease is signed, so owners can visit.

What's the difference between pre-seed and a planted tree?

A planted tree costs KSh 4,000 and is already in the ground when you choose it. A pre-seed tree costs KSh 3,500 and is reserved before planting. We plant it within 90 days, or refund you in full. Your money is held in a separate account until it's planted.

When will my tree give fruit?

Grafted Hass trees usually start fruiting in their third or fourth year. KALRO puts a worthwhile commercial crop at around year six, and full bearing follows. See the yield table above for estimates.

Do I pay anything after I get the tree?

No. The farm company's 40% share of the fruit covers rent and care. If its costs run higher, the company carries the difference.

What happens if my tree dies?

A reserve tree of the same age on the same acre takes its place, and your name moves to it on the register. You'll see the change on your tree's record.

Can I give or pass on my tree?

Yes. We'll record a transfer to another person, and the new owner's name goes on the tag and register.

What about tax?

If you sell your fruit or your tree, you are responsible for any tax due to KRA on that income. We'll give you a yearly statement of your harvest to help.
