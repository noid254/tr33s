Before you plant

What can go wrong, in plain words

Trees are living things. We would rather you know the risks now than find them later.

Trees can die

Drought, pests or disease can kill a tree. Reserve trees replace losses, but a large loss could use them up.

Harvests vary

Weather and the age of the tree change how much fruit it gives. Some years will be thin.

Prices move

Avocado prices change with season, exchange rates, shipping costs and export rules.

Fruit takes years

First fruit comes in year three or four, and the first harvests are small.

Nothing is guaranteed

We do not promise a fixed amount of fruit or money, or that a tree will be worth more later.

Estimates are estimates

Our figures come from sourced yield ranges and 2026 price quotes. Real results will differ.

How we estimate

Yields use ranges for a well-managed grafted Hass tree: none in years 1 to 2, rising from 4 to 10 kg in year 3 to 100 to 160 kg from year 8 through year 30. KALRO puts orchards older than five years at 80,000 to 100,000 fruits per hectare, roughly 670 to 830 fruits per tree at 120 trees per hectare. We then take your 60% share.

Prices per kg are assumptions taken from 2026 sources that disagree. One source lists a Kenyan farm-gate average near KSh 19 per kg (FAOSTAT, June 2026). Another lists KSh 150 to 180 for export-grade fruit at peak season and KSh 35 to 50 off-season. We use KSh 35 for the cautious case, KSh 80 for the middle case (our blended assumption) and KSh 150 for the strong case. Grading, season and the buyer all move the real figure, and costs of sale are not deducted.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can I plant a tree today?

Our first plots are opening soon. Leave your details on the Plant a tree page and we will tell you the moment your trees are ready. Nothing is paid until then.

What will it cost?

From KSh 4,000 per tree, paid once, with no monthly fees. The farm company's 40% share of the fruit covers land rent, water, labour, inputs, security and harvesting. If its costs run higher, the company carries the difference.

What do I actually own?

You own a specific tree, with its code, tag and GPS pin registered in your name, and a right to 60% of its fruit under your agreement. The land stays leased by the farm company.

When does my tree give fruit?

Grafted Hass trees usually begin fruiting in their third or fourth year. KALRO puts a worthwhile commercial crop at around year six. See the estimate table above.

How do I receive my share?

You can collect fruit at the farm, have it delivered at cost, or take the sale value of your share. Your agreement sets out the details.

What happens if my tree dies?

A reserve tree of the same age in the same block takes its place and your name moves to it on the register. You will see the change on your tree's record.

Can I give a tree as a gift?

Yes. Choose the gift option, add the recipient's name and an occasion, and the name goes on the tag.

Can I pass on or sell my tree?

You can transfer your tree to another person, and the new owner's name goes on the tag and register. We cannot promise a buyer or a price.

Where is the farm, and can I visit?

Our orchards are in Narok County, on land held on a long lease. Owners can book farm visits and walk to their own tree.

What happens at the end of 30 years?

Your agreement sets out the terms for the end of the 30-year term, including whether it can be renewed. Trees often keep producing well beyond that.

What about tax?

If you sell your fruit or your tree, you are responsible for any tax due to KRA on that income. We give a yearly harvest statement to help.
