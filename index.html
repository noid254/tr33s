How we estimate

Yields use ranges for a well-managed grafted Hass tree: none in years 1 to 2, rising from 4 to 10 kg in year 3 to 100 to 160 kg from year 8 through year 30. KALRO puts orchards older than five years at 80,000 to 100,000 fruits per hectare, roughly 670 to 830 fruits per tree at 120 trees per hectare. We then take your 60% share.

Prices per kg are assumptions taken from 2026 sources that disagree. One source lists a Kenyan farm-gate average near KSh 19 per kg (FAOSTAT, June 2026). Another lists KSh 150 to 180 for export-grade fruit at peak season and KSh 35 to 50 off-season. We use KSh 35 for the cautious case, KSh 80 for the middle case (our blended assumption) and KSh 150 for the strong case. Grading, season and the buyer all move the real figure, and costs of sale are not deducted.

Why a tree is more than timber

It heals the land while it grows your share

A planted tree holds soil, gives shade and cools the ground, and Melia does it where little else will grow.

Made for Kenya's dry lands

Melia is one of the trees KEFRI promotes for arid and semi-arid lands (ASAL). It grows on 300 to 800 mm of rain a year and farmers plant it to stay productive through droughts. We plant at the start of the rains in large water-catching pits, so there is no routine watering. It is low-maintenance, not no-maintenance: it still needs weeding, pruning and protection from browsing animals in the early years.

Soil and shade

Its leaves rot into the soil and add nutrients, and its shade helps ground hold moisture. Farmers report degraded land recovering under it.

Wood that lasts

Timber turned into floors, doors and furniture keeps its carbon locked away for as long as it is used. Termites and decay avoid this hardwood.

Wind and erosion

Rows of trees break the wind and slow runoff, which protects the soil around them.

Kenya needs more trees

Kenya's national goal is 10% tree cover, and the country imports a large share of its wood needs. Every tree helps.

What is Melia volkensii?

A drought-tough East African hardwood, known in Kenya as Mukau. Its timber is hard, durable and resistant to termites and decay, and is used for furniture, doors, window frames, rafters and floors.

What will it cost?

KSh 1,000 per tree, paid once, with no yearly fees. The farm company's 30% share of the timber covers land rent, planting, fencing, labour, security and harvesting. If costs run higher, the farm company carries them.

What do I actually own?

You own a specific tree, with its code, tag and GPS pin registered in your name, and a right to 70% of its timber value at harvest under your agreement. The land stays leased by the farm company.

How long until harvest?

About 12 years. KEFRI advises harvest from around age 12, and a tree on a poorer site can take up to 18 years to reach a good size. We wide-space the trees so none is cut early, and we tell you when your batch is ready.

Where will it grow, and will you water it?

We will plant Melia in dry areas where it grows naturally, inside the 300 to 800 mm of yearly rain KEFRI recommends, and announce the site once the land is secured. We plant at the start of the October to December rains, in pits at least 45 cm wide and deep that hold rainwater, so we do not plan routine watering. The trees still need weeding two or three times a season for the first three years and protection from goats and wild browsers for the first one to two years. If a tree fails we replace it. We report survival and growth on your profile, and the 12 years is a target, not a guarantee.

How do I receive my share?

At harvest we saw and sell the timber and pay you 70% of the proceeds after sawing, or deliver your share as boards at cost. Your agreement sets out the details.

What happens if my tree dies?

A reserve tree of the same age in the same block takes its place and your name moves to it on the register. You will see the change on your tree's record.

Are permits needed to harvest and sell?

Yes. The Kenya Forest Service requires a permit to transport forest products, including from private land. We arrange it at harvest, and permit and transport costs come out of the proceeds before the 70 / 30 split.

Can I give a tree as a gift?

Yes. Choose the gift option, add the recipient's name and an occasion, and the name goes on the tag. A tree planted today is a gift for 12 years from now.

Can I pass on or sell my tree?

You can transfer your tree to another person, and the new owner's name goes on the tag and register. We cannot promise a buyer or a price.

What about tax?

If you receive money from your share of the timber, you are responsible for any tax due to KRA on that income. We give a harvest statement to help.

Twelve years is long

Nothing is paid before the harvest. The money comes at the end, not along the way.

Rules can change

Harvest and transport permits and fees are set by the government and can change.

Nothing is guaranteed

We do not promise an amount of timber or money, or that a tree will be worth more later.

Why adopt

Most seedlings die from neglect. Adopted trees have someone watching.

Kenya is planting trees on a huge scale, but planting is the easy part. What decides whether a tree lives is care in its first two years. Every tr33s tree has a farm team looking after it and an owner watching it.

Planting is counted, survival isn't

The government's JazaMiti app records about 738 million trees, against more than a billion reported planted, and those counts track planting, not survival.

Care is what works

Trees in places with dedicated care, like schools and churches, survive at much higher rates than trees left alone. That care is what you adopt.

How we choose the land

  • Highland land within the Wambugu apple's range of about 1,200 to 2,100 m.
  • Water we can rely on, from a river, borehole or storage.
  • Farmers nearby who can grow with our seedlings and sell with us.
  • Our first site: riverfront land in Mweiga, Nyeri, in an established farming area.

What we manage

  • Water. Apples need about 800 to 1,200 mm of rain a year. Where rain falls short, drip irrigation and storage come first.
  • Pests. Fruit flies, aphids, thrips and powdery mildew are the main threats. The farm team checks and treats the trees all year.
  • The land. A title search, a lawyer and a registered lease before a single tree goes in.

Companies, schools, churches, chamas and NGOs can adopt a row or a whole block, for staff, clients, members or a community you care about. Every tree gets a name on its tag, and you get one shared report with photos.

Staff and clients A named tree for every team member, or a thank-you gift that keeps growing.

Schools and churches A tree for every class or family, and harvest days to share.

Chamas and groups Adopt together and follow your trees in one place.

Events and campaigns A tree for every guest, sale or milestone.

We plant it. We care for it. You watch it grow. We harvest together.

Leave your details and we will tell you the moment adoptions open. It takes about three minutes, and nothing is paid today.
